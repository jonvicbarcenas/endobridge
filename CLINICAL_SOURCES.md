# Clinical data sources

EndoBridge records observations for clinician review; it does not diagnose PCOS, diabetes, or dyslipidemia. Glucose and lipid findings are metabolic risk context, not PCOS-specific findings. An OB/GYN or other treating clinician selects tests, interprets results, and decides whether referral is needed.

## Standards represented in the form

- [ACOG, Polycystic Ovary Syndrome](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/polycystic-ovary-syndrome-pcos): clinical context for PCOS and its metabolic associations.
- [2023 International Evidence-based Guideline for PCOS, hosted by ASRM](https://integration.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/recommendations-from-the-2023-international-evidence-based-guideline-for-the-assessment-and-management-of-polycystic-ovary-syndrome/): lipid profile at diagnosis includes total cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C, and triglycerides (1.7.3). The 75 g OGTT is the most accurate glycemic test in PCOS (1.9.9); fasting plasma glucose or HbA1c can be used when OGTT is unavailable, with lower accuracy (1.9.10). Clinically available insulin assays are not recommended for routine care (1.9.12). Total and free testosterone are primary biochemical hyperandrogenism assessments; DHEAS is only a conditional consideration if they are not elevated (1.2.1-1.2.2). AMH is not a stand-alone PCOS test (1.5.3).
- The same PCOS guideline defines irregular cycles in adults more than three years after menarche as intervals under 21 or over 35 days, or fewer than eight cycles a year (1.1.1). The questionnaire records timing for clinician review; it does not infer ovulation or diagnose PCOS.
- [ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026, diagnosis and classification](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S27/163926/2-Diagnosis-and-Classification-of-Diabetes): BMI >=23 kg/m2 is a diabetes screening threshold for adults of Asian ancestry when other risk factors are present. This is a screening threshold, not a PCOS diagnostic cutoff.
- [CDC, Diabetes Testing](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/diabetes-testing/index.html): fasting plasma glucose 100 mg/dL, HbA1c 5.7%, and 2-hour OGTT glucose 140 mg/dL begin the prediabetes ranges used for observation flags. A result needs clinical interpretation and often confirmation.
- [American Heart Association, Cholesterol and Lipids](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/cholesterol/your-complete-guide-to-understanding-cholesterol-and-lipids) and [Metabolic Syndrome](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/metabolic-syndrome/symptoms-and-diagnosis-of-metabolic-syndrome): the general lipid screening context (total cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C, triglycerides).

The active study panel is the ten clinician-selected values: total testosterone, triglycerides, fasting glucose, total cholesterol, HDL-C, LDL-C, TSH, free T3, free T4, and HbA1c. There is no glucose-test selector or lab reference minimum/maximum input. Hormone and thyroid values are recorded for clinician review without an automatic high/low label; this avoids implying a universal cutoff for assay-dependent measurements. The printed lab units must match the form's units. Uploaded result values with a different unit are never copied directly into the form.

The prior record formats remain stored and readable. New sessions require weight and height for BMI calculation and all ten study-panel values. This is a local study protocol, not a claim that ACOG mandates these exact ten tests. The international PCOS guideline recommends the full lipid profile included here and prefers a 75 g OGTT for glycemic assessment; fasting glucose and HbA1c are less accurate alternatives when OGTT cannot be performed. The app makes no claim that most people with PCOS have diabetes or high cholesterol.
